# HRT brief: predictions and decisions

*Hack the Burgh XIII, Knack (built as Understudy). Written for the Hudson River Trading judges, "best use of data for predictions and decisions". We could not find the official brief, so this is written to the lens a quantitative judge uses: probabilistic forecasts scored with proper scoring rules, honest out-of-sample tests with baselines and intervals, no leakage and no forking paths, decisions judged by realised cost against naive and oracle policies, and a plain account of when the model fails.*

**Every number in this document is generated.** `scripts/hrt_brief.py` reads it from `evals/report.json` and the other files under `evals/` (a test fails if the committed text differs from a fresh render, and if a sentence that says "beats" or "loses" stops being true). **All data is synthetic or simulated**, from generators we wrote, and each result says so. The live study (n = 4 teammates) has not run. Nothing here is a claim about real workers.

## 1. In thirty seconds

1. We forecast three things and use them to decide: whether someone makes no progress in the next minute (stuck), how long until the next progress event, and whether an agent's answer is wrong. The decisions are when to speak, which cases a person reviews, when to start background work and when to build an automation.
2. **Predict first, score later.** Every forecast is written to a hash-chained ledger before its outcome exists, with the forecasts of the naive predictors beside it, and scored only from that record, on seeds nobody had used. In the world the model was fitted for, its log loss minus that of the better naive predictor ({{hl:worlds/v1/model_vs_best_naive/baseline|base}}) is {{hl:worlds/v1/model_vs_best_naive/est|s3}} nats per bin {{hl:worlds/v1/model_vs_best_naive/ci|ci3}} (negative is better).
3. **When the world changes the model gets worse than a one-line baseline, and we say so.** On three structurally different generators it is worse than the best naive predictor by {{hl:worlds/markov/model_vs_best_naive/est|s3}} (markov), {{hl:worlds/latent_skill/model_vs_best_naive/est|s3}} (latent skill) and {{hl:worlds/process_trace/model_vs_best_naive/est|s3}} (process trace) nats per bin. A guard that watches the ledger raised its flag in {{hl:worlds/markov/guard/flags}} of {{hl:worlds/markov/guard/of}} deployments (markov), {{hl:worlds/latent_skill/guard/flags}} of {{hl:worlds/latent_skill/guard/of}} (latent skill) and {{hl:worlds/process_trace/guard/flags}} of {{hl:worlds/process_trace/guard/of}} (process trace), and in {{hl:worlds/v1/guard/flags}} of {{hl:worlds/v1/guard/of}} where nothing had changed. Falling back changed log loss by {{hl:worlds/markov/guard/strategies/guarded_minus_always_model/est|s3}}, {{hl:worlds/latent_skill/guard/strategies/guarded_minus_always_model/est|s3}} and {{hl:worlds/process_trace/guard/strategies/guarded_minus_always_model/est|s3}} nats against always trusting the model (negative is better), and by {{hl:worlds/v1/guard/strategies/guarded_minus_always_model/est|s3}} where nothing had changed.
4. **Decisions are scored against naive and oracle policies on fresh simulated worlds**, four registered contrasts at the Bonferroni-adjusted level. The shipped rules beat their naive comparison in {{hd:wins}} of 4, **and lose to a naive policy in one place we print next to the win**: building an automation at once is cheaper than our build-timing rule at the pack's own build costs.
5. **Of {{hl:claims/#len|i}} registered claims, {{hl:claims/#passed|i}} passed and {{hl:claims/#failed|i}} did not** (section 2b). The model's calibration intercept in the world it was fitted for is {{hl:worlds/v1/scores/predictors/model/calibration/intercept|s3}} {{hl:worlds/v1/scores/predictors/model/calibration/intercept_ci|ci3}}: it under-predicts the stuck rate slightly, and the interval excludes zero. The error-risk model's calibration slope is {{hl:worlds/route_no_drift/scores/predictors/model/calibration/slope|f3}} {{hl:worlds/route_no_drift/scores/predictors/model/calibration/slope_ci|ci3}}: slightly over-confident. <!-- assert hl:worlds/v1/scores/predictors/model/calibration/intercept_ci/0 > 0 --> <!-- assert hl:worlds/route_no_drift/scores/predictors/model/calibration/slope_ci/1 < 1 -->
6. What did not hold up is in section 3 and in the README's "What did not hold up". The edge of the stuck model over "time since progress" was mostly the generator; a simple rule does about as well under mismatch; on the real-model agent runs a one-bit indicator predicts false claims better than our model.

## 3. What a quant judge will attack, and our answer

**Leakage.** *Attack:* a model predicting "no progress in the next minute" from event streams can leak the label through its features or through the split.
*Answer:* the features are causal (a test truncates the episode at each bin and rebuilds them; identical). The split in the prospective part is in time: the models are fitted once on a training pool, frozen, and every forecast is appended to the ledger before its outcome exists; the ledger refuses a prediction older than the newest entry and an outcome earlier than the horizon; the online baseline reads only outcomes the ledger has already resolved (a test recomputes it from the record). The held-out seeds are disjoint from every earlier evaluation (a test). *What we do not claim:* the v1 world draws new people of the four persona types the model was fitted on; the label is defined from the same `progress` events that the generator emits.

**Generator dependence.** *Attack:* a model fitted and tested on our own generator measures the generator.
*Answer:* we tested that, on purpose, and report it. {{rt:findings/id=realism-generator-dependence/text}} The prospective part repeats it on new seeds. Registered claim P3, that in the shifted worlds the deployed model is worse than the best naive predictor in at least two of three families, {{hl:claims/id=P3/pass|held}}: markov worse = {{hl:claims/id=P3/evidence/markov/worse|yn}}, latent skill worse = {{hl:claims/id=P3/evidence/latent_skill/worse|yn}}, process trace worse = {{hl:claims/id=P3/evidence/process_trace/worse|yn}} (worse means the interval of model minus best naive lies above zero). Its calibration slope there is {{hl:worlds/markov/scores/predictors/model/calibration/slope|f2}}, {{hl:worlds/latent_skill/scores/predictors/model/calibration/slope|f2}} and {{hl:worlds/process_trace/scores/predictors/model/calibration/slope|f2}}: not merely over-confident but, where negative, pointing the wrong way. The accepted v2 stuck model (A4) is a small gain on held-out people and, under mismatch, a one-feature rule does better than it (section 5).

**Small n.** *Attack:* four people.
*Answer:* yes. The stuck-model headline is leave-one-person-out over four synthetic people and says so; its interval resamples episodes, so read the per-person spread. The live study (n = 4 teammates) has not run and its design cannot show conventional significance: the smallest possible two-sided p is {{r:study_power/min_p_by_n/4|f3}}. The prospective part uses {{hl:worlds/v1/n_people}} persona-streams of four types for the v1 world and {{hl:worlds/markov/n_people}} people per shifted world, with people as the bootstrap cluster, and says that the 32 are eight draws of four types.

**Multiple comparisons.** *Attack:* you tried many things and report the good ones.
*Answer:* the plan for this part was committed at 00:33 GMT on 8 Nov, before any number existed; it fixes every claim, margin, seed and parameter, runs held-out once through a command that refuses without a freeze, with a changed code hash or a second time, and keeps a dated amendment log (A2 records a plan statement that a dev run showed to be wrong). The guard's other settings are an exploratory table, never a claim. The decisions scoreboard registers a family of four and reports it at the Bonferroni-adjusted level; every other cell is labelled exploratory with the count. ML v2 counted every variant (`evals/mlv2/PLAN.md`) and its held-out result is mostly negative (the README says so).

**Simulated data.** *Attack:* none of it is real.
*Answer:* correct, and each section carries the badge. What it shows is that the machinery recovers planted structure, and how it behaves when the world changes; the live study is the step that would show more, and it has not run. The oracle policies in the scoreboard use the generator or the future: they bound what is possible and are not implementable.

**Calibration under shift.** *Attack:* a calibrated model on training data is miscalibrated in use.
*Answer:* measured prospectively: see the slope and intercept per world above and in the reliability table on the page. The guard handles one failure (the model is no better than a naive predictor). It does not handle another: in the error-risk world after a rule change the model stays better than the naive predictor ({{hl:worlds/route_drift/second_half/model_vs_online_base/est|s3}} {{hl:worlds/route_drift/second_half/model_vs_online_base/ci|ci3}}) while its calibration intercept moves from {{hl:worlds/route_drift/first_half/calibration_model/intercept|s2}} to {{hl:worlds/route_drift/second_half/calibration_model/intercept|s2}}; the guard is silent, correctly by its own rule, and nothing recalibrates the model. That is a gap (section 6).

**Decision value against prediction accuracy.** *Attack:* a better log loss does not make a better decision.
*Answer:* we score the decision, not the forecast. Where they come apart we print it: the build-timing rule uses a forecast and still loses to "build at once" at the pack's own costs; the review router's value depends on what an escaped error costs, and it is the cheapest of the three plans (review nothing, route, review everything) only for a cost between {{hd:rows/1/cost_ratio/routing_beats_reviewing_nothing_when_an_escaped_error_costs_more_than_minutes|f0}} and {{hd:rows/1/cost_ratio/routing_beats_reviewing_everything_when_an_escaped_error_costs_less_than_minutes|f0}} minutes of expert time; the start rule beats "run whenever idle" at the loss ratio the 10% interruption cap implies and the margin changes with that ratio (cells in the scoreboard); the stuck alarm overshoots its own false-alarm budget ({{r:hazard/false_alarms_per_hour|f2}} an hour {{r:hazard/false_alarms_per_hour_ci|ci2}} against {{r:hazard/budget_false_alarms_per_hour|f1}}).

## 7. Reproduce

cd services/engine
python -m pytest tests/hrt -q                                   # ledger, scoring, guard, worlds, replay, scoreboard, freeze, sections
python -m understudy_engine.hrt.run dev                         # DEV pools only, writes evals/hrt/results/dev_*.json
